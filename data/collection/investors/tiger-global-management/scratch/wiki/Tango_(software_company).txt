Tango Technology, Inc., commonly known as Tango, is an American software company that develops a digital adoption platform for documenting, guiding and automating browser-based workplace processes. Its software records actions performed in web applications and converts them into editable step-by-step guides containing screenshots and descriptions.
The company was founded in 2020 by Ken Babcock, Dan Giovacchini and Brian Shultz. Tango publicly launched its browser extension in September 2021. In April 2025, the company launched an artificial intelligence service for automating parts of browser-based workflows.


== History ==
Tango was founded in 2020 by Ken Babcock, Dan Giovacchini and Brian Shultz, who left Harvard Business School to develop the company. Its initial product was a browser extension that recorded users' workflows and generated step-by-step documentation with screenshots.
Before its public launch in 2021, Tango tested the extension with pilot customers. At the time, the product focused on employee training, particularly for customer-support and sales teams, rather than workflow automation. In August 2021, after initially being financed by its founders, Tango announced a $5.7 million seed funding round led by Wing Venture Capital.
Tango publicly launched its browser extension in September 2021. By June 2022, the software was available as both a Chrome extension and a desktop application. That month, the company introduced Workspaces, a shared library in which teams could organize, edit and share recorded workflows and folders. Tango also announced a $14 million Series A funding round led by Tiger Global Management.
In April 2025, Tango launched an artificial intelligence service that expanded the platform beyond workflow documentation to include automation of browser-based tasks.


== Software ==
Tango uses a Chrome extension to record the steps performed by a user in a web-based application. It captures individual actions and screenshots, adds action labels and generates a step-by-step guide. Generated guides can be edited, shared through a link, exported as a PDF or embedded in webpages and wikis. 
Tango's "Guide Me" mode presents a generated guide as an interactive overlay within a web application while the user performs the documented task. Its automation service can perform selected steps in browser-based workflows. The service accepts workflow rules written in natural language and allows individual steps to be designated for either automated or manual completion.


== Reception ==
In 2022, Tango was named a Webby Awards honoree in the Apps and Software: Productivity, Financial Services & Banking category. Google also included the Tango extension in its list of favorite Chrome extensions of 2022.
