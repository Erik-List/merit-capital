Turning a Continuous Improvement Mindset Into Successful KPIs - TCV Podcast: Growth Hacks Turning a Continuous Improvement Mindset Into Successful KPIs Marty Sanders, Head of American Sales at Vectra, chats with TCV Principals, Katja Gagen and Kunal Mehta. August 30, 2021 Share When growing and scaling sales teams, stasis can set in even quicker than a sales leader might realize. That’s why Marty Sanders, head of American sales at cybersecurity leader Vectra, prioritizes continuous improvement as a strategy and goal for every member of his organization. During this episode of Growth Hacks, the trio discuss how Marty hires sales leaders that are hyper focused on growth, and how Vectra supports their employees with a culture that allows them to excel. Marty also walks us through how his new hires generated 50% of revenue in just six months&#8230;and how they cut their sales cycle by more than half along the way. Here’s what you will learn: Why Vectra treats recruitment with the same focus they do dealmaking and sales How to recruit and hire talent that has an improvement-focused mindset The strategy behind implementing a culture of continuous improvement How Vectra shortened its sales cycle while growing revenue generated by new hires To hear more on this, settle back and press play. It gives us back a lot of time because it&#8217;s customized for us, and it&#8217;s able to take into consideration the skills and the wills that makes sense for the candidate and it makes sense for us here at Vectra and we don&#8217;t have any exceptions. You pass the test, or we don&#8217;t move on. Kunal Mehta: Marty, I love, just the science that you apply in terms of hiring. We spend so much time training sales reps on qualification. Marty Sanders: Absolutely, because now we&#8217;ve done a good job at sourcing candidates, and we get somebody that passes the test. There&#8217;s still a lot of work to do so in closing the sale because it is a sale, and we use MEDDIC as part of our hiring process. Just making that offer goes a long way in the recruitment of a candidate. Kunal Mehta: And what&#8217;s the result at Vectra through this process? Marty Sanders: Well, I adopted it as soon as I joined the company about seven and a half months ago, and the last two quarters, in what used to be considered a long sales cycle, over 50% of our revenue is coming from folks that have been hired in the last six months. A lot of our companies in the portfolio have very long sales cycles and then they hire these industry superstars with a big Rolodex, and they pay non-recoverable draws. What&#8217;s your take on that? Marty Sanders: My take is this is a problem that many companies are facing, where we hire the person with the expensive Rolex and the nice suit, and they make all these big promises and nine months go by and you really don&#8217;t have the right scorecard to see if they&#8217;re doing a good job or not. &nbsp;So, I address it in a couple of ways. Number one is what can we do to shorten that long sale cycle? And I&#8217;m obsessed with that. And making sure when I use the sales, like a DNA test or personality profile, does this person have the ability to do transactions? Even though you&#8217;re not a transactional business, just having that transactional ability really goes a long way in shortening sales cycles. Number two is building a scorecard to measure if they&#8217;re doing a good job and then three, and most importantly, tying that draw to the KPIs on that scorecard. I will not give a draw just for the sake of giving a draw, that you get money just for showing up. We&#8217;re going to make sure we tie that draw to MBOs that are great leading indicators that we&#8217;re building pipeline. But even activities like identifying stakeholders, meeting the stakeholders, getting the senior leadership like me or my CEO in front of the customer. Identifying use cases, modeling a TCO, developing unsolicited proposals. And most importantly, have you identified pain in any part of an organization? Just making sure we&#8217;re paying that draw and tying it to hitting these KPIs. Kunal Mehta: Marty, I think those verifiable outcomes that you&#8217;re talking about and tying the draw payment to that is incredibly refreshing to hear. I know you have this great way to onboard, ramp, even identify talent, but you also have an incredible way to think about just continuous improvement as folks come in. Maybe you can talk to that a little bit and how you&#8217;ve compressed things like sales cycle. Marty Sanders: Continuous improvement has to be throughout an entire organization, and everybody has to be obsessed with that. Probably my biggest obsession right now, is shortening the sales cycle. When I came to Vectra, our sales cycle in America was 241 days, and now we&#8217;re down to 111. Last quarter we had five new customer transactions, brand new net, new customers that closed in less than 30 days. So, we&#8217;re certainly trending in the right direction. And the pillars to get that done is &#8212; a big part of it is culture and not allowing that limiting belief that all of our sales cycles are nine to 12 months. One of the things that you have to do again, going back to the sales DNA, is making sure you hire people that do not have that limiting belief and agree with me that we can shorten that sales cycle. Number two is making sure all your sellers can articulate the value prop in 90 seconds or less. You can get that in less than 90 seconds; you can build a 90-day sales cycle. Next is building a culture of compression where every Friday is treated like month end, every month end is treated like quarter end, and every quarter end is treated like year-end. By the way, this is tough. It can wear people out, but you have to maintain it. But because it&#8217;s tough, you have to do two things. Again, hiring people who are built for that. And then number two, really being aggressive in recognition and making sure that you&#8217;re checking in on your people, that they can continue to maintain that pace. Kunal Mehta: That level and that mindset is incredibly important. And I remember Marty, when we first met, you talked about no goose eggs ever. Maybe you can just explain to our listeners how that mindset is so critical in your own organization. Marty Sanders: A great example is, you know, a sales rep who doesn&#8217;t clearly have a deal to commit, you know, from a forecasting perspective. And they&#8217;ll say their commit is zero. You cannot allow anybody to put a goose egg in their commit. They must put something . Something has to be in there because if you allow them to have a zero forecast, you&#8217;re basically giving them a mulligan. You&#8217;re giving them the subconscious right to fail. By getting them to commit to something, anything, even if it&#8217;s $50K, that commitment now is tied to a moral obligation, and they are morally obligated to get to that commit. So, it&#8217;s very important that none of your managers can ever accept somebody committing to zero. Katja Gagen: So, it&#8217;s a lot about having growth in mind, but also being very focused. Marty Sanders: I would probably start with, are you using the proper pre-framing techniques before you message to a customer? Here&#8217;s one of my favorites. The action may be a deeper dive, talk to a reference and some cases they just want to accelerate the move to a procurement, but 75% of the people who see what I&#8217;m going to share with you, take action.&#8221; And at the end of my presentation, I&#8217;m not going to have happy talk. I&#8217;m going to say &#8220;based on what we just went through Kunal share with me your next action. I&#8217;m not asking you; I&#8217;m telling you to share with me your next action.
