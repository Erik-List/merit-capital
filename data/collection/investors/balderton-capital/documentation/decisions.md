# Balderton Capital — investor-specific decisions

Applies the membership rules of `../../../COLLECTION.md` to Balderton Capital
(slug `balderton-capital`, display name always **Balderton Capital**; "Benchmark
Capital Europe" / "Benchmark Europe" from 2000 until the independence of June
2007). The judge cites these rulings per line. Written at kickoff 2026-09-26
(PRO-106, sub-issue of the PRO-87 wave) under the derivation protocol of
COLLECTION.md §1: **derivations** (a standing test applied to verified facts; no
confirmation needed, vetoable), **deltas** (Erik's), and **run-time standing
rulings** (dated, class-wide, flagged for review). PRO-106 named one delta, the
Benchmark Europe boundary; it is derived below (D2) from facts the Benchmark run
(PRO-88) already ruled on and from the UK register, and is applied, flagged.

**The structural fact of this run:** Balderton is a London Series A firm whose
portfolio is European and whose companies file nothing with the SEC, while its
own fund side is visible there (fifteen fund filers, one exempt reporting
adviser with a Section 7.B table of sixteen private funds, and fund-level
ownership filings on the US listings: Fund IV's 13Gs, Fund VI's 13Gs). Its
first seven years were spent as the London desk of Benchmark Capital, whose
own site listed the desk's companies (PRO-88 preserved those rows for this
run). What replaces the portfolio-side Form D: (1) the firm's own record read
in depth — the live list of 205 cards, each carrying the firm's own entry round
and year, a Live/Exited status, a location and a sector; the news archive (552
posts); the archived listing eras 2007–2026 once the Internet Archive answers
(under a machine-wide cooldown at kickoff); the benchmark.com Europe desk pages
2001–2009 — (2) the exit-side EDGAR full-text and Wikipedia sweeps, and (3) the
UK register: Companies House gives the firm's own entity history, the exact
tenure of every partner (the LLP's designated members), each partner's
directorships with dates and service addresses (the officer graph, Atomico's
D9), and a free status flag per UK company.

- **D10 — The UK officer graph is corroboration with a date, not membership
  alone (§1 evidence rules, T4; Atomico D9).** A director appointment of a
  Balderton person inside their tenure is the register's analogue of a Form D
  related-person seat: it corroborates a listed row and dates its entry, and it
  is **affirmative** for an unlisted company only when the appointment's
  service address is a Balderton office (20 Balderton Street W1K 6TL to 2015;
  The Stables, 28 Britannia Street WC1X 9JF from 2015) or the seat passes
  between two Balderton people, and the company resolves to a real operating
  business (the a16z director-of-record narrowing). A seat with neither is a
  lead for the recall probe, never a verdict. Seats in a person's own vehicles
  (family companies, the firm's own entities, the dormant service companies)
  are not rows. Company status from the register (dissolved, liquidation,
  administration) is filing-grade `defunct` / `acquired` evidence for UK rows
  (COLLECTION.md §5, "registers pay off on status").

- **D11 — The firm's own per-company fields.** Each live card publishes the
  firm's **round label with a year** ("Seed in 2015", "Series A 2016",
  "Secondary in 2020"), a **Live / Exited** status, a **location** ("London,
  UK") and a **sector**. The round label is eligible by semantics — it is the
  firm's claim about the round it entered, beside the year, not the company's
  current stage — and it labels (`site_api.entry_stage_fields: ["stage_site"]`,
  `first_party`) only after it is scored blind against the book's documented
  rounds (≥95% over ≥30 rows for `high`, `medium` below); a documented round
  overrides it with `stage_disputes_band`. Words that name no round are anchors
  only: the ten bare-year labels ("2008"), "Initial investment", "Investment",
  "Pre-Series A" (straddles two stages), and "Acquired Rahko" (D6). The year is
  a CLOSE-year anchor for `first_seen` and the censor clock, never an
  `entry_date` on its own (Menlo finding). The Live/Exited flag is status
  evidence overridden only by a cited event (Point Nine R3); the location is
  first-party `hq_city` / `hq_country` evidence, current per the firm.

  **Scored 2026-09-26, at the taxonomy level.** Against dated announcements

## Run-time standing rulings (dated, class-wide, flagged for Erik's review)

- **R1 — A minority entry that later became a control holding stays a member on
  the entry** (2026-09-26, agent; T3 control rule applied at the transaction
  level). Codemasters: the desk bought 40% in 2005, a further 30% in 2006 and
  the founders' remainder in 2007 (GameSpot). The 2005 purchase was a minority
  position at scale, a Growth entry; the later transactions took control. The
  row is a member on the 2005 entry, `entry_stage: Growth`, and is raised for
  Erik: the alternative reading excludes the whole position as a buyout.
- **R2 — A company the desk owned outright, alone or with one partner, is a
  control position, not a member** (2026-09-26, agent; T3, Erik 2026-09-04).
  Alphyra: Benchmark took it private in the EUR 88.6M management buyout of
  April 2003 and held 63% of the acquisition vehicle (Private Equity
  International, Irish Times). NASN: a joint venture of Benchmark Capital
  Europe and Setanta Sports, sold by the two to ESPN in December 2006 (Walt
  Disney Company release). Both are `not_member` with
  `exclusion: control_position` (the reason code the Insight Partners run added
  to the builder, 6fdf0fc), preserved in `excluded.json`. The firm's own card
  lists NASN as "Series A in 2005"; raised for Erik because the rule removes
  two desk-era exits from the track record.
