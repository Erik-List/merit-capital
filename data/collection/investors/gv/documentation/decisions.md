# GV: investor-specific decisions

Applies the membership rules of `../../../COLLECTION.md` to GV (slug `gv`,
display name always **GV**; "Google Ventures" for the firm before its December
2015 rename). The judge cites these rulings per line. Written at kickoff
2026-09-29 (PRO-75) under the derivation protocol of COLLECTION.md §1:
**derivations** (a standing test applied to verified facts; no confirmation
needed, vetoable), **deltas** (Erik's), and **run-time standing rulings**
(dated, class-wide, flagged for review).

**The structural facts of this run.** GV is Alphabet's venture arm, not an
independent manager. It has no adviser registration of any kind, its funds are
dated vehicles (Google Ventures 2011, GV 2013 to GV 2023, GV 2025) filled by
its parent, and its vehicles file no Form D because there is no offering. The
firm's own site is a public Sanity dataset that lists 646 companies with the
deal partners and the firm's own exit line, keeps its exits (243 starred), and
publishes a second list of 112 AI companies with its own dated investments.
The base is therefore the easiest part of this run and the profile the
hardest (the issue's point): three §11 fields assume a Form ADV that does not
exist.

## Derivations (T1 to T5 applied to verified facts; vetoable, no confirmation needed)

- **D1: The firm and its vehicles (T1).** Facts, verified 2026-09-29:
  - **No adviser registration.** IAPD returns no firm for GV, GV Management,
    GV Management Company, Google Ventures, Google or Alphabet. **GV
    Management Company, L.L.C.** (CIK 1940173) files only 13F notices (its
    13(f) holdings are reported inside Alphabet Inc.'s 13F) and N-PX, from
    2022.
  - **The vehicles** (EDGAR submissions, all at 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway,
    Mountain View): **Google Ventures 2011, L.P.** (CIK 1586699; general
    partner Google Ventures 2011 GP, L.L.C., 1639805), **GV 2013, L.P.**
    (1634261, filed first as Google Ventures 2013, L.P.), **GV 2014, L.P.**
    (1728797), **GV 2015, L.P.** (1719755), **GV 2016, L.P.** (1721789),
    **GV 2017, L.P.** (1733340), **GV 2019, L.P.** (1845041), **GV 2021,
    L.P.** (1861202) and **GV 2023, L.P.** (1961979), each with its GV 20xx GP
    entities, which file 13D/G and Forms 3/4 as holders; **Google Ventures
    2012, L.P.**, which never filed under its own name but is named in its
    portfolio companies' filings (Upstart's 2020 S-1/A holder table, DocuSign's
    2018 registration with the Mountain View notice address, a Nextdoor
    management rights letter of 2012-10-05; found by the recall triage); and **GV 2025 GP,
    L.P.** (2036706), whose Form D (2025-01-10, amended 2026-01-08) reports an
    indefinite offering, $0 sold, 78 then 84 investors under 3(c)(1) and
    David Krane as executive officer: the team's own general-partner vehicle,
    not outside limited partners.
  - **The firm's own statements** (gv.com CMS, read 2026-09-29): "EST. 2009",
    "$13B Under Management", "650 all-time investments", "400 Active Portfolio
    Companies", "230+ M&A", "80 IPOs"; offices San Francisco Bay Area (the
    headquarters), New York, Cambridge and London.
  - **Alphabet's other investing units.** CapitalG ("Alphabet's independent
    growth fund ... Our sole LP", capitalg.com) has its own vehicles on EDGAR
    (CapitalG 2013 LP to CapitalG IV LP, and CapitalG LP, filed first as Google
    Capital 2016, L.P.), its own team and its own brand, at the same address.
    Gradient ("investing at the forefront of artificial intelligence since
    2017", its own "$220M Fund 5", gradient.com) raises its own funds. Google
    and Alphabet also invest from the corporate balance sheet (strategic
    stakes), and Alphabet's own companies (Calico, Verily, Waymo, Wing) are
    subsidiaries, not investments.

  **Outcome: IN** for a position held by any GV vehicle, and for every company
  the firm lists as its own (its /portfolio or /ai list, any archived era):
  one firm under one name since 2009, the Google Ventures years included. The
  investments Google Ventures made before its first L.P. existed (2009 to
  2010) were made by the same team with the same parent's money, and the firm
  lists them as its own, so its listing suffices (§1; the Sutter Hill D7
  reading of a firm's pre-archive book). **External:** CapitalG and Google
  Capital (a separate firm with its own fund family; the Meritech and
  Accel-KKR precedents), Gradient, Google's and Alphabet's corporate
  investments, Alphabet Inc. and its holding companies (they co-file GV's
  13D/G as the parent; the registry must never crawl them, or CapitalG's and
  Google's positions would import), and the "GV" namesakes (GV SPV I is a
  series of Growthline Ventures Master LLC; GV Black Capital, GVR and the
  like are unrelated advisers).

- **D2: Fund money (T2).** Alphabet is the firm's only source of capital; the
  test is whether a GV vehicle, or the Google Ventures team investing its
  allocation before the vehicles existed, held the position. **Outcome:** a
  position that only Google, Alphabet or CapitalG held is not a GV position,
  even where press writes "Google invested". Checked at kickoff: Magic Leap,
  SpaceX, Anthropic, Lyft, Airbnb, CrowdStrike and Duolingo, names the press
  ties to Google's or CapitalG's money, are on neither of GV's lists; a row
  for any of them needs a GV vehicle filing or a source naming GV itself.
  Calico is an Alphabet company, and the firm does not list it; with no GV
  vehicle on record it is out (a membership trap, since Wikipedia files it
  under "GV companies"). A partner's personal angel investment is out (the
  Kushner and Cherry precedents): a company a partner backed personally needs
  a GV position of its own.

- **D3: Venture entry (T3).** Primaries, follow-ons, secondaries and tenders
  IN, tenders as Growth. A position that first appears as a post-IPO 13G or
  13D with no documented pre-IPO round is out (the Lightspeed and Thrive
  rule). GV runs no buyout practice, so the control-position addendum is not
  expected to fire; a PIPE is judged on its own facts.

- **D4: Positions in Alphabet's own companies (T3, the control addendum).**
  The firm lists Waymo (partners David Krane, Elena Sakach) and Isomorphic
  Labs (Krishna Yeshwant), companies Alphabet controls, which sell priced
  rounds to outside investors. The control addendum excludes a transaction in
  which the firm, alone or with its co-investor group, **acquires** control;
  GV acquired none (Alphabet's control predates and survives every round),
  and the rounds are real financings, so every round column means what it
  means elsewhere. **Outcome: IN**, as a minority financing position the firm
  lists as its own, with the company `private` at its latest priced round
  (§6 already reads Waymo that way). Flagged for Erik as a class (the report
  counts it), because the parent's control is a fact no other book has had.

- **D5: Person or firm (T4).** A seat is dated against the person's firm at
  filing time. GV's partners arrive from Google itself and from operating
  jobs, and several left for firms of their own; the live CMS carries current
  staff only (its bios name prior roles: Michael McBride at GitLab, David
  Schenkein at Agios, Issi Rozen at the Broad Institute). The roster
  (`scratch/build-roster.py`) dates each person from the archived people pages
  on googleventures.com and gv.com and from the live CMS; a Form D seat outside
  the window is the person's, not the firm's. Departures are read from the
  capture windows, not from memory. The roster landed at 68 people
  (`sources/wayback/partner-roster.json`), each year dated by that year's own title on the person's page
  (Kevin Rose and Rich Miner kept pages for years after they left, retitled as advisors); the build
  removed 7 Form D rows whose only evidence was a seat filed four to nine years after the person left.

- **D6: Operating companies only (T5).** Fund-shaped entries on any list are
  `not_member` until identified as operating companies (the standing rule);
  none is expected on GV's lists, which carry companies only.

- **D7: The /ai list's dates are anchors, not labels.** Each /ai company
  carries the firm's own rounds as "Invested" or "Re-invested" with a month
  and year. The first "Invested" date is the firm's own statement of when it
  entered: it sets `first_seen` and serves as the `entry_date` clock where no
  round source dates the entry more precisely, and it bounds the search for
  the entry round. It names no round, so it never mints a stage (§6, "a bare
  year is an anchor, not a label").

- **D8: The site's exit line is dated evidence, not status.** The firm writes
  "Acquired by Nebius, 2026" or "NASDAQ: ME" on its exited companies. It names
  acquirers and tickers and corroborates `status`; the column itself is
  current state and is judged per row (23andMe's ticker survives its 2025
  bankruptcy and asset sale, for one).

- **D9: Survivorship.** The live list keeps exits but, on the evidence at
  kickoff, drops shutdowns: Homejoy (closed 2015) and Brandless (closed 2020),
  both GV-backed, are absent from both lists. The archived listings on
  googleventures.com (Common Crawl from 2012) and gv.com recover them, and a
  company the firm listed in any era is a member by that listing (§1).

- **D10: An investment company on the list is a fund position (T5).** The
  firm has listed Oxford Sciences Innovation since 2015 (renamed Oxford
  Science Enterprises in 2021), and its own 2015 listing describes it as "a
  fund that provides capital and business expertise to companies developed in
  Oxford University". **Outcome: `not_member`**, the LP-stake class, because
  the firm's own label says the position is an interest in an investing
  vehicle (the Sutter Hill D6 reading of a firm's own "investment
  partnerships"); every slug of its merge group carries the verdict.

- **D11: Sale consideration (§1).** The GV vehicles' filings on Starco Brands
  (Forms 3/4 and 13D/G from February 2023) are the stock Starco paid for
  GV-backed Soylent, which the firm lists as "Acquired by Starco Brands,
  2023". **Outcome:** Starco Brands `not_member` (the a16z Starco precedent),
  Soylent the member. **The same holds where the firm lists the acquirer
  itself** (Erik 2026-09-22, the Greylock ruling): the firm's own line for
  Revolution Medicines reads "NASDAQ: RVMD, Acquired EQRx 2023", and it
  appears on the list only from 2025, with no partner, after Revolution
  Medicines paid for GV-backed EQRx in stock. **Outcome:** Revolution Medicines
  `not_member`, EQRx the member. The recall triage found the same class in
  filings: Twilio (for Segment), Qualcomm (for Modular, 2026), Mondee (for
  Rocketrip), Neumora (for BlackThorn) and Pluralsight (for Smarterer), none
  of them on GV's lists.

## Deltas (Erik's)

- **Delta 1 (open): the profile of a manager with no Form ADV.** §11 anchors
  `aum` on Item 5.F or the Section 7.B gross assets, and `hq_location` on the
  ADV's principal office. GV has neither. What the record does hold: the
  firm's own "$13B Under Management" (first-party, not sworn), Alphabet's 13F,
  which reports GV's public holdings only, and the firm's own headquarters
  label ("San Francisco Bay Area", not a municipality). The run applies the
  treatment the issue asked for as a proposal (fields with no sworn source
  published only as the firm's own statement, flagged, or null) and raises
  the contract for corporate venture arms at close. It blocks nothing in the
  base.

## Run-time standing rulings (dated, class-wide, flagged for review)

- **R1 (2026-09-29): stock received in a merger is sale consideration.** Where a GV-backed company merges
  into another and GV's stake becomes shares of the combined company, with no new GV money named, the
  combined company is `not_member` and the GV-backed company stays the member (the §1 sale-consideration
  rule, Erik 2026-09-22, read for a merger). Applied to Seedling (Moonfrye, renamed P.S. XO, merged into it in
  October 2014; the new $7M named Upfront and Greycroft, not GV). Flagged: the parties called it a merger of
  equals.
- **R2 (2026-09-29): two GV positions that merge are one row.** Chroma Medicine and Nvelop Therapeutics
  (both GV-backed) combined as nChroma Bio in December 2024; the firm lists the successor, so the three rows
  are one (`identity-merges.json`). The entry is read through the predecessor the firm files the successor
  under (Issi Rozen, Chroma's partner, on the live list), and the other predecessor's entry is recorded in the
  note, not as a second entry.
- **R3 (2026-09-29): an entry the firm re-links in place is one company.** The 2016 to early-2019 "Sense"
  entry linked sense.com (a home energy monitor) with that company's description; in May 2019 the same entry,
  in the same slot and colour, was re-linked to sensehq.com. sense.com's own investor list has no GV and the
  firm's /ai list has SenseHQ investments from 2017, so the earlier link is the firm's error and the archive
  holds one company, not two (the curated split was removed from `scratch/cc-parse.py`).
- **R4 (2026-09-29): team, patents and a technology licence together are a sale.** Nebius took Clarifai's
  core team, bought its patent portfolio and licensed its technology (May 2026), and the firm's own exit line
  reads "Acquired by Nebius, 2026": the row reads `acquired`, because the business's core changed hands (the
  asset-sale reading of §6), not the acqui-hire rule, which covers a hire without the business. Flagged for
  Erik: some press calls it an acqui-hire, and the three sibling rows (Lux, Menlo, USV) read private.
- **R5 (2026-09-29): a venture-creation firm on the list is a fund position (D10).** Curie.Bio's $520M
  from GV, ARCH and a16z funded a seed-investing vehicle and a services unit ("a new biotech VC", Fierce
  Biotech), so it reads `not_member` under D10 at medium confidence. Flagged: a16z's row keeps it as a
  member.
