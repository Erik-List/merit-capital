# NEA: investor-specific decisions

Applies the membership rules of `../../../COLLECTION.md` to New Enterprise
Associates (slug `new-enterprise-associates`, display name **NEA**). The judge
cites these rulings per line. Written at kickoff 2026-09-30 (PRO-100) under the
derivation protocol of COLLECTION.md §1: **derivations** (a standing test
applied to verified facts; no confirmation needed, vetoable), **deltas**
(Erik's), and **run-time standing rulings** (dated, class-wide, flagged for
review).

**The structural facts of this run.** NEA is one registered adviser founded in
Baltimore in 1977, investing from one numbered fund family (New Enterprise
Associates I to 19) across technology and healthcare, with a growth-equity
sleeve (NEA 18 and 19 Venture Growth Equity), an opportunity fund, single-deal
SPVs and, since 2024, a secondaries fund. Its own site is a Statamic CMS whose
GraphQL endpoint answers without a token and lists 917 companies with the
firm's own first-investment year, a stage label, the status and exit line, the
NEA deal team and the NEA board members. The archive adds the companies the
live list has dropped (1,018 company pages on Common Crawl since 2013, 186 of
them absent from today's list) and the pre-2008 book (Internet Archive only).

- **D2: Fund money (T2).** A position is NEA's when a fund of the firm held
  it. **Outcome:** partners' personal angel checks, a venture advisor's or
  special partner's own board seat and an LP position in another manager's
  fund are OUT (Sequoia, a16z, Accel, Lightspeed, Cherry, Point Nine
  precedents). A company listed on NEA's own site is IN on the listing (§1),
  unless the firm's own page or the record shows the money was not NEA's (the
  Menlo partner-carried rule).

- **D3: Venture entry (T3).** Primaries, follow-ons, secondaries and tenders
  in a private company are IN, secondaries and tenders as Growth. **The
  issue's premise that NEA "moved portfolios into continuation vehicles" is
  wrong on the facts**, and it re-derives under the same tests: NEA
  Secondary Opportunity Fund is not a continuation vehicle for NEA's own
  funds but a secondaries fund the firm could raise only once it registered
  as an RIA in 2023 (TechCrunch, 2024-08-28). **Outcome:** a company whose
  first NEA position is a direct secondary bought by that fund is a member
  entering at Growth (T3); an LP interest it buys in another manager's fund
  is not a row (T5). The one real transfer of NEA positions, the 2018
  NewView spinout, moved stakes out of NEA's funds to another firm: the
  companies stay members through NEA's original entries, and NewView's own
  later investments are external (T1). NEA runs no buyout practice; a control
  transaction, if one surfaces, is OUT under the control addendum.
  **PIPEs** are the delta below.

- **D4: Person or firm (T4).** Standard person-level attribution and seat
  dating. NEA carries a large bench beside its partners (venture partners,
  special partners, venture advisors, EIRs); a seat any of them held is
  dated against the person's firm on that date and counts only where an NEA
  fund held the position.

- **D5: Operating company (T5).** Fund-shaped entries on the firm's own
  list (the archived `indous-ventures` page: NEA's stake in the IndoUS joint
  venture; any other fund or program page) are `not_member` until identified
  as operating companies. Vehicles, feeders and SPVs are never rows.

- **D6: What the site's fields mean (§5, §6).** `first_invested` is the
  firm's own year of first investment: it sets `site_invest_year`, an anchor
  for `entry_date`'s year, never a round label. `company_stage` reads Seed,
  Early, Growth or Public/PIPES and describes NEA's investment (a company's
  current stage is never "PIPES"), so it is **eligible**; but `Early`
  straddles Seed and Series A and so is an anchor only (the firm uses it for
  Series B leads too: Cloudflare 2010, Robinhood 2015, Duolingo 2012), and
  `Public/PIPES` names no round. Whether `Seed` and `Growth` label a row is
  decided by the score against documented rounds, and a documented round
  overrides the label with `stage_disputes_band` either way. **Scored
  2026-09-30** (`scratch/stage-score.json`): Seed 15/21, Growth 20/25, 76%
  together, so both label at **medium** confidence (COLLECTION.md s5: the score
  sets confidence, not admission). The misses run one way by size: `Seed` on
  small first-priced Series A rounds (TiVo, Data Domain, Tamr, Synlogic),
  `Growth` on large Series A rounds (MoonPay, Desire2Learn, Supernus, Monte
  Rosa), and two plain mislabels (Tempus at its Series C, Pair at its B).
  `Early` resolved to Series A 8 times and to Series B or later 6 times
  (Workday, Elastic, Robinhood, BuzzFeed): it never labels. The status field and exit line ("NYSE: NET", "Acquired by
  PayPal") corroborate and go stale.

- **D7: The old book (policy constant).** There is no entry-date window
  (PRO-137, 2026-09-19). The issue text still describes the retired 2006
  window; it is superseded. A 1980s entry is judged on the same evidence as a
  2024 one, and where the record does not name the round the stage reads
  `undetermined`. The pre-2006 share is reported, not chased.

- **D8: Sale consideration (§1, Erik 2026-09-22).** Where NEA's list carries
  an acquirer whose stock the funds received for selling a portfolio company
  (the archived `salesforce-com` page beside Tableau and MuleSoft, both sold to
  Salesforce), the acquirer is `not_member` with the acquired company named.
  *Correction at kickoff:* the archived Salesforce.com page is not an
  acquirer page; it states "Year invested 2002, growth", two years before
  Salesforce's listing, so it is judged as a claimed private position. The
  case that does apply: **Upwork**, which the firm lists with a 2003 first
  investment, the year of its Elance entry; NEA's Upwork stock came from
  oDesk's 2013 all-stock combination with Elance, so Upwork is `not_member`
  and Elance is the member (the GV Revolution Medicines and Seedling
  precedents).

## Delta (Erik's; the run proceeds on the reading below, flagged)

- **X1: PIPEs.** The firm labels 52 listed companies `Public/PIPES`: its own
  statement that the investment was a private placement into a listed
  company. The open question is the one Coatue R1 and TCV X1 raised: whether a
  negotiated PIPE is a venture entry. The run proceeds on their reading,
  **a PIPE into an already listed company is a listed purchase and out**
  (T3), per row: where the row's own record shows an NEA private round before
  the listing, the row is a member on that round whatever the label says. If
  Erik rules a PIPE a venture entry, the affected rows enter as Growth.

## Run-time standing rulings

(dated, class-wide, flagged for review)

- **R1: A company NEA forms to buy a business is judged for control (T3
  control addendum), 2026-09-30, flagged.** The recall triage found two:
  Dialysis Newco, formed by NEA 13 with Frazier in 2011 to buy the 28 clinics
  DaVita had to divest (the firm lists it as DSI Renal), and NEA Wind
  Acquisition Corp., which with NEA Management Company bought Northern Power
  Systems out of its parent's insolvency in 2008. Where the record shows NEA
  and its co-investors acquiring control, the row is out under
  `control_position`; where NEA took a minority in a financing, it is a
  member at Growth. Decided per row in the grind.

- **R2: The Growth Equity Opportunities vehicles' 13D issuers are the X1
  class, 2026-09-30.** Every company those vehicles report on (Geron,
  VistaGen, Savara, Celcuity, Gossamer, Addex, Verona, Affimed, AVEO,
  Cascadian, Satsuma, NeuroMetrix) was listed when the vehicle bought in, and
  most carry the firm's own `Public/PIPES` label. They are judged under X1: out
  unless an NEA private round came first.

- **R3: A medical-device incubator's numbered vehicle is not a company; the
  company it spins out is (T5, D5), 2026-09-30, flagged.** NEA lists Josh
  Makower's ExploraMed incubator vehicles (ExploraMed II, IV and V) and The
  Foundry's vehicle (Foundry Therapeutics 1, "the premier medical device
  company incubator" in the firm's own words). *Corrected 2026-09-30 (fix pass
  F2):* Foundry Therapeutics 1 is Allay Therapeutics under its earlier name,
  the same EDGAR filer (CIK 1766366), so it is a company and merges into
  Allay; ExploraMed IV and V and Prospex Medical III stay out. Each funds several device
  companies in turn, so it is the fund-shaped class: `not_member` until a
  source shows it is itself an operating company. The numbered NewCos those
  incubators form are companies, and each is judged on its own record
  (ExploraMed NC7 is Willow, NC8 is Revelle Aesthetics; both merged into the
  names the firm lists).

- **R4: An affiliate fund NEA co-general-partnered but that ran from its own
  office is external (T1, the Meritech reading), 2026-09-30, flagged for
  Erik.** Three 1980s-90s funds carry an NEA-named general partner beside
  their own: Spectra Enterprise Associates (principal office NEA's own, 1119
  St. Paul Street, Baltimore: IN, re-derived above), Chemicals & Materials
  Enterprise Associates (office Cleveland, individual general partners
  Baruch and Murfin, co-GP NEA Chemicals and Materials Partners) and
  Southwest Enterprise Associates (office 14305 Inwood Road, Dallas, general
  partners C. Vin Prothro and NEA Partners Southwest, 13G of 2000-02-14). The
  test that decides it is where the fund itself was run: CMEA and SEA are
  external. **The rows it moves:** Dallas Semiconductor, founded in 1984 on
  SEA's money with Prothro as its chairman, reads `not_member` (NEA's own
  entry is the firm's 1990 `Public/PIPES` position, after the 1987 listing;
  X1). If Erik reads NEA's co-general-partnership as enough, SEA and CMEA
  become NEA sleeves and Dallas Semiconductor enters in 1984.

- **R5: A venture syndicate's carve-out newco is a control position under
  the addendum as written, 2026-09-30, flagged for Erik.** TriVascular (TV2
  Holding bought the business from Boston Scientific in March 2008; NEA 12
  held about half the Series A that paid for it) and Xcel Pharmaceuticals
  (founded in January 2001 to buy Elan's Diastat and Mysoline lines; Domain and
  NEA 10 held about 54%) are both out as `control_position`: the co-investor
  group acquired the whole business. Both were otherwise ordinary Series A

  back as members at Series A (their classifications already read that way).

- **R6: NEA's own company formations and single-sponsor platforms,
  2026-09-30, flagged for Erik.** Where NEA alone funded a company formed to
  buy operating businesses, the row is read as control: American Pathology
  Partners (a $75M NEA equity commitment in 2008, NEA the only investor named,
  then three lab acquisitions) is out. Where NEA led a syndicated financing,
  the row is a member: Curana Health (NEA led a $300M round with Oak HC/FT and
  others, 2021). NEA's co-investments beside Frazier Healthcare's buyouts are

  Health, Comprehensive Pharmacy Services, RevSpring), as are Paladina Health

- **Re-derived 2026-09-30 (a wrong prior, same test): Connect Ventures is
  IN (T1).** The recall brief treated Connect Ventures, the partnership of
  Creative Artists Agency and NEA (2021), as its own vehicle and out. NEA's
  own Form ADV (CRD 161004, amendment of 2026-08-26, Section 7.B) lists

- **R7: A pre-1998 Schedule 13G by a fund dates the holding before the
  listing (X1 evidence), 2026-09-30, flagged for Erik.** Before the 1998
  passive-investor rule, a holder that was not an institution could file
  Schedule 13G instead of 13D only if it acquired the shares before the class
  was registered or bought at most 2% in any 12 months. So an NEA fund
  reporting 5% or more on a 13G at the first year-end after an IPO held the
  stake before the listing, even where the IPO prospectus predates EDGAR.
  GeneMedicine (NEA V, 1994 IPO) and Penederm (NEA IV, 1993 IPO) are members
  on this reading, stage undetermined. Platinum Software (NEA VI, a fund
  raised after the 1992 listing) and Aquila Biopharmaceuticals (a 1998
  purchase) stay out under X1. If Erik reads the filing route as too weak, the
  two rows leave.
