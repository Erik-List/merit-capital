# Seedcamp — investor-specific decisions

Applies the membership rules of `../../../COLLECTION.md` to Seedcamp (slug
`seedcamp`, display name always **Seedcamp**). The judge cites these rulings
per line. Written at kickoff 2026-09-30 (PRO-83) under the derivation protocol
of COLLECTION.md §1: **derivations** (a standing test applied to verified
facts; no confirmation needed, vetoable), **deltas** (Erik's), and **run-time
standing rulings** (dated, class-wide, flagged for review). PRO-83 asked for
one ruling explicitly: the accelerator programme against the fund holding. It
is derived below (D2, D3) from the firm's own announcements, and one part of it
(the warrant-only option) is raised as a delta.

**The structural fact of this run:** Seedcamp is a London pre-seed and seed
firm (founded May 2007 by Saul Klein and Reshma Sohoni) whose first seven
years were a selection programme — Seedcamp Week and the Mini Seedcamp events
across Europe — that invested a standard cheque on standard terms in the teams
it picked, and which became a seed fund from 2014. Its companies are European
and file nothing with the SEC; it takes **board observer roles, not board
seats** ("we do not take board seats but we do typically take up board
observer roles", seedcamp.com/faqs, 2026-09-30), so neither the Form D
related-person layer nor the UK officer graph carries its positions (both
measured at kickoff, D8). What carries the book: (1) the firm's own record —
the live list of 327 companies with the firm's investment year per company,
the archived lists 2008–2022 (read from Common Crawl while the Internet
Archive is under its machine-wide cooldown), and the Views archive of 1,337
posts back to 2007 whose cohort posts name every programme investment; (2) the
exit-side EDGAR full-text and Wikipedia sweeps; (3) the UK register for status.

- **D2 — The programme and the fund (T2, fund money; PRO-83's ask).** From 2007
  to 2017 Seedcamp selected teams at events — Seedcamp Week in London (and from
  2014 in Berlin) and the one-day Mini Seedcamps across Europe, Tel Aviv and
  Mumbai — and **invested in some of them**. The firm's own posts keep the two
  apart every time: "These Are The 22 Startups Attending Seedcamp Week London"
  (2015-02-03) is followed by "Seedcamp Family Grows to Over 150 as 11 New
  Startups Join" (2015-02-16: "we're excited to announce our investment in the
  following startups who attended Seedcamp Week London"); the September 2014
  week had 21 attendees and "Announcing 11 New Seedcamp Investments"
  (2014-09-18); the 2007 page lists "this year's six Seedcamp companies". So:
  - **A company named in a "join", "new investments", "new teams" or
    "Seedcamp companies" post, or on any list of the firm's companies, is a
    member**: the programme cheque was fund money (Seedcamp Investments LLP,
    II LLP, then Seedcamp III), the same T2 fact that put YC's batch deal,
    a16z speedrun and Accel Atoms in.
  - **"Joining the Seedcamp family" is the firm's word for an investment**
    ("after investing in two teams at Seedcamp Budapest, we hit another great
    milestone: exceeding 75 investments", 2012-11-09). From 2011 the Mini
    Seedcamp events fed investments directly ("Mini Seedcamp Ljubljana – Two
    new teams join Seedcamp", 2011-08-10; "16 of 20 [Seedcamp Week teams] were
    already Seedcamp Winners of Mini Seedcamp events", 2011-09-12), so a 2011+
    Mini Seedcamp team the firm welcomes into the family is a member. The
    **2009 Mini Seedcamp "winners" were not investments** (below); 2010 is read
    post by post.
  - **A finalist, an attendee, or a Mini Seedcamp "winner" that the firm never
    names as an investment is OUT** (SimilarWeb, "won the first Israeli
    SeedCamp" in its Wikipedia article, received an invitation and cloud
    credits; decisions D3): an event place is not a position
    (Lightspeed's Extreme Entrepreneurs, no funding; Balderton Launched). The
    finalists and attendee posts are not parsed as lists (`scratch/cc-parse.py`
    reads only the winners and companies surfaces), and a name that surfaces
    only there is judged `not_member`.
  - Mentors, sponsors, the "Seedcamp Nation" founder community, Seedsummit and
    the Seedcamp Academy put no money in anyone: never rows.

- **D3 — The programme's standard deal is `Pre-Seed` (programme terms, Erik
  2026-09-19).** Where a company entered on the programme's standard cheque,
  the stage is the programme's: `Pre-Seed`, source `program_terms`, the
  citation **the terms document of its era**, the note naming the cohort post.
  The terms, verified from the firm's own pages:
  - 2014–2017 (Seedcamp III): "Seedcamp will continue to invest in three
    ways: €75K for 7% / 3% as a Warrant / Up to €200K into a seed round between
    €300K and €2m" (post "Your First Round Fund", 2015-09-21,
    seedcamp.com/views/your-first-round-fund/; the same three options are the
    site's "Platform" menu 2015–2017). The firm calls the first "our pre-seed
    investment" (post of 2016-06-02, "850+ applications for our pre-seed
    investment") and counts the third separately: "over 70 investments, 24 of
    them at Seed" (2015-09-21).
  - 2007–2009 (Fund I): "we will select 5 teams out of the 20 to receive an
    investment of €50K per team ... in exchange for a small minority stake"
    (seedcamp.com/pages/about_faqs, captured 2008-07-19).
  - 2010–2014 (Fund II): "up to €50K per team ... in exchange for a small
    minority stake (8%-10%) in the company with a straightforward set of
    terms" (the same FAQ captured 2010-09-02; seedcamp.com/faq 2012-02-04 and
    2014-03-07).
  The capture URLs are in `sources/site/programme-terms.json`. Seedcamp Week
  winners were the invested teams ("These teams will all receive a €50k
  investment from Seedcamp", Seedcamp Week 2009 post); Mini Seedcamp winners
  were not ("All 3 teams are invited automatically to the Mini Seedcamp of
  their choice ... A special bundle of Amazon Web Services credits", Tel Aviv
  2009; "will be presenting ... to Seedcamp's investment committee who will
  consider making an investment", London 2009). The firm's own count is a
  recall check: "the 22 Seedcamp companies from 2007-2009" (FAQ 2012); the
  winners pages give 6 + 7 + 8 = 21.
  - 2017–2020 (Seedcamp IV): "We will lead your first investment with a
    cheque of £100K for 7.5% equity. We will co-invest in seed rounds of up to
    £2M as long as you have a lead investor" (seedcamp.com/faq, captured
    2017-12-14, 2018-12-11, 2019-05-23 and 2020-06-06). The first is a
    standard cheque on standard terms (programme terms); the second is a seed
    round led by another investor. By 2020-11-26 the answer is a range
    ("around £200K, usually ... between 6% – 7%") and by 2020-12-01 "Our first
    cheque is usually between $350K-$1M": from late 2020 there is no standard
    deal.
  **A Fund I or Fund II entry with no cohort post is still a programme entry**:
  until Seedcamp III (September 2014) the programme's standard cheque was the
  firm's only instrument (the FAQs of 2008–2014 describe no other), so a
  company first listed by the firm before September 2014 (its 2012–2014
  /companies/ list, or an investment year before 2014 on the live list
  confirmed by an archived listing) takes `program_terms` at `medium`,
  the era's terms page cited. A live-list year alone, with no archived
  listing to confirm it, does not date an entry (Codacy: "2012" on the live
  list, absent from every list before 2018) and the stage reads
  `undetermined`. From 2014 the firm ran the standard cheque beside
  negotiated seed investments, so a 2014–2020 row enters on programme terms
  only where a cohort post or the firm's own words name the standard deal,
  and on `Seed` only where the record names a seed round; otherwise
  `undetermined`.
  A company the firm announces as a **seed** investment in that era ("Since
  launching our new fund we've been actively making seed investments as well",
  2015-02-16: MarcoPolo; "Seedcamp joins $2.5m seed investment in TheWaveVR",
  2016-08-05) is **not** a programme entry: its stage is the round the record
  names (`Seed`), cited to the post. From 2018 (Seedcamp IV on) there is no
  standard deal — the firm leads or joins negotiated pre-seed and seed rounds
  ("Our first cheque is usually between $350K-$1.25M", FAQs 2026) — so every
  entry takes the round the record names, from the firm's post first, then
  press; where no source names the round the stage is `undetermined`. The live
  list's "Year of Investment" is an anchor, never a round (COLLECTION.md §6).
  A programme entry leaves the S2A cohort (a Pre-Seed entry is not a Seed
  entry), so this book's S2A is measured on the named seed entries.

- **D4 — Instruments (T3).** Primaries, follow-ons, SAFEs and the firm's own
  ASA (Advance Subscription Agreement, the UK convertible it publishes as its
  "standard pre-seed ASA") are IN at the round they fund; secondaries and
  tenders IN as Growth entries (none expected). The firm's **sale** of part of
  its TransferWise stake (January 2017) is an exit, not an entry. Post-IPO or
  public purchases with no venture entry OUT; LP stakes OUT; control positions
  and sponsor promotes OUT (none expected at this cheque size). Evidence
  sufficiency unchanged: a listing on any era of the firm's own site suffices
  alone, a post naming the investment suffices alone, a fund vehicle on an SEC
  filing suffices alone, a name-match never.

- **D10 — The Views archive is a first-party surface** (Creandum ruling 8,
  Balderton D12). seedcamp.com/views (1,337 posts, 2007–2026, read through the
  WordPress REST index and each post page; `sources/site/views-posts.json`)
  names programme cohorts, seed investments and portfolio rounds. A post is
  class-1 evidence for the round it NAMES and asserts an entry only where it
  says so in words ("our investment in Liveblocks' $1.4M pre-seed round",
  "why we led pre-seed into Tickitto"); a portfolio company's later round
  announced on the blog is a follow-on (COLLECTION.md §6, the Menlo finding). A
  company named in a post as a Seedcamp investment is `site_listing`-grade
  membership evidence.

- **D11 — The UK register** (COLLECTION.md §5; Balderton D10, R3). Company
  status from Companies House (dissolved, liquidation, administration) is
  filing-grade `defunct` / `acquired` evidence for UK rows, the company page
  its citation. A confirmation statement naming a Seedcamp vehicle as
  shareholder is `fund_filing` membership evidence, read per company where a
  row needs it; a list that could not be read is recorded as failed, never as
  "not a holder".

- **D12 — FX and non-US public rows (policy constants).** Every GBP / EUR / SEK
  / other ECB-currency figure converts at the ECB reference rate of its own
  date via `scripts/fx-convert.py`, the native figure in the note; LSE (Wise)
  and other non-SEC listings take a web-cited market cap; a US-listed row
  (UiPath) keeps the SEC float layer as corroboration. No entry-date window
  (PRO-137).

## Deltas (Erik's)

- **E1 — A warrant-only position ("3% as a Warrant", 2014–2017).** The firm
  offered a warrant over 3% of the company as one of its three ways in,
  beside the €75K-for-7% cheque. A warrant granted for access to the platform
  is a position the fund holds without having put money in. The membership
  definition requires an investment with fund money, so the derivation
  applied from kickoff is **OUT unless a fund of the firm later invested cash**
  (then a member at that entry), flagged per row; the alternative reading keeps
  such companies as members at `Pre-Seed`. Raised for Erik with the results,
  with the count of rows it touches (the firm's posts rarely say which option a
  company took).

**String and namesake traps:** a Seedcamp Week attendee or finalist announced as
"joining us" for the week, not the portfolio (D2); Mini Seedcamp "winners"
(D2); the LP and corporate names on `/investors/` (D7); the acquirer labels
(D5); Velti (D5); the Tom Wilson and Will Bennett Form D clusters (D8); the
AngelList syndicate SPVs (D6).

## Run-time standing rulings (dated, class-wide, flagged for Erik's review)

- **R1 — An acquirer's name on a sold company's card is not a member (D5,
  2026-09-30).** The live list shows "Taxfix" with investment year 2018 and
  TaxScouts' own line ("Digital tax advisor that offers online tax preparation
  services"); the archived lists carry TaxScouts under that line, and

  shows a separate investment in Taxfix, so the holding is the consideration
  for TaxScouts: `taxfix` is `not_member`, TaxScouts the member, `acquired`.
- **R2 — A renamed company is one row (2026-09-30).** The firm renames a card
  when the company renames, keeping the logo slot, so the same logo-upload
  month and the same one-line description across two names is one company.
  Merged in `companies/identity-merges.json`: Legal Tender to Apperio, API
  Changelog to Hitch, Homeshift to HomeHero, Garmz to LOOKK, Koia to Mava,
  Integration.app to Membrane, Nanoget to Propoly, Milk.ly to Milkster (the
  card title), Stupeflix's card shown as Replay, ActionBar to Simpo, TPH to
  Hack your Closet, BaseKit's card shown as SiteJam, Teleportd to Totems,

- **R3 — A cohort post that mixes pre-seed and seed teams reads as the
  programme's standard deal (D3 applied, 2026-09-30),** unless the firm's own
  words show the company past it: Finance Fox sat in the January 2016 post
  after "a $5.5M Series A ... in 2015" (the firm's post of 2016-04-19), so its
  entry round is `undetermined`.
- **R4 — A Fund II-era card without a cohort post (D3 applied).** A company on
  the 2012-2014 `/companies/` lists, or whose logo the firm uploaded before
  September 2014, enters on the Fund II terms at `medium`. Try.com rests on
  its logo upload month alone (2012-08; the lists read carry it only from
  2018) and is the borderline case.
- **R5 — A fund commitment is not a company (D4).** "Foreword Fund ... An
  early-stage micro fund led by Declan Kelly" on the live list is an LP stake:
  `not_member`.
- **R6 — The firm's own posts are a recall layer (D10 applied).** Eight
  members appear in the Views archive only (Arcane, Kern AI, MindLabs,
  Ourspace, Recurse ML, Salesroom, Specify, Troop): on neither the live list
  nor any archived list read. They entered as recall additions with the post

- **R7 — A company bought and then liquidated is `defunct`.** Cymmetria:
  Stage Fund bought it in September 2019 and its employees filed for its
  liquidation that December (Calcalist). Aligned with the Felicis book; the
  YC book's `acquired` is aligned in this run's ingest.
